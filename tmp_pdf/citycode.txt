ABSTRACT
Urban spatial intelligence calls for 3D cities that agents can measure, interpret,
and modify across successive tasks. Maintaining such cities as persistent, code-
based states requires lightweight acquisition and support for consistent state up-
dates without repeated dense reconstruction. We therefore formulate a new setting
for grounded urban world construction: generating large-scale, instance-level pa-
rameterized 3D cities from one low-altitude aerial RGB image per region and
known camera parameters, while recovering metrically scaled buildings and ex-
plicit road geometry and connectivity. We introduce CityCode, an agent-operable
representation that unifies metric geometry, urban topology, visual semantics, and
3D assets through persistent entity identities, with dependency-aware versioned
execution for validated updates and historical restoration. We further develop
Air2Code to construct this representation by combining visual structure extrac-
tion with adapted metric depth estimation. To address occlusion and incomplete
coverage, recovered structure and observed appearance guide generative comple-
tion and subsequent structure-aware 3D generation for buildings and backgrounds.
Experiments on CitySample-Aerial and UrbanScene3D demonstrate substantially
more accurate metric structure recovery and superior visual fidelity to the tar-
get cities compared with the evaluated baselines. Across 50 task chains of 50
steps each, CityCode supports continued querying, editing, planning, and histor-
ical restoration over evolving city states. Together, CityCode and Air2Code turn
sparse aerial observations into persistent, agent-operable urban worlds for long-
horizon urban spatial intelligence.
1 INTRODUCTION
Urban spatial intelligence calls for agents that can understand and act on 3D environments across
successive tasks. For a specific city, this includes locating buildings, measuring their geometry,
reasoning about road access, and modifying layouts, etc. These operations require connecting the
city’s metric geometry, road connectivity, and visual semantics, etc. (Zhang et al., 2026; Caldarelli
et al., 2023). As edits accumulate, subsequent queries and plans must remain consistent with the
updated entities and spatial relationships. These needs motivate constructing agent-operable 3D
cities: structured environments built from observations of a target city and maintained across tasks
for continued spatial reasoning and action.
A key challenge is constructing such worlds from limited input. Design-driven approaches, includ-
ing CityGenAgent, CityCraft, and MajutsuCity, generate programs, layouts, and assets from user

Image-conditioned methods such as Sat2City v2, Sat2Density++, Sat3DGen, MagicCity, and
SceneFuse-3D generate or reconstruct urban scenes from aerial or satellite imagery (Hua et al., 2026;
Qian et al., 2026a;b; Yao et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025). Their primary focus is scene-level syn-
thesis or reconstruction, rather than jointly recovering metrically parameterized building instances,
explicit road connectivity, and entity-linked assets for subsequent spatial operations. Sparse aerial
views leave substantial facade appearance and occluded geometry unobserved. Constructing a us-
able city from these views requires coordinating structure recovery with generative completion:
synthesized details must remain compatible with recovered massing, location, and metric scale.
We introduce a new setting for grounded urban world construction: generating instance-level pa-
rameterized 3D cities from sparse aerial observations for continued spatial reasoning and action.
Given one low-altitude aerial RGB image per region and known camera parameters, the goal is to
jointly construct measurable building geometry, explicit road connectivity, and detailed appearance
(Figure 1). Our central idea is to use recovered metric entities as a common interface between obser-
vations, generated assets, and spatial operations. Each building retains one identity linking its visual
evidence, metric structure, and generated asset across construction and subsequent tasks.
To realize this idea, we introduce CityCode, an agent-operable representation linking metric urban
structure, observation evidence, visual semantics, and 3D assets through persistent entity identities.
2
Air2Code constructs this representation by combining visual structure extraction with adapted met-
ric depth estimation (Figure 2). Its structure-and-observation-guided generation completes geometry
and appearance from sparse observations, combining appearance completion and structure-aware
3D generation for buildings with height-guided background generation. During subsequent interac-
tion, CityCode’s dependency-aware versioned execution validates supported edits, updates affected
records, and enables queries, planning, and historical restoration.
Experiments on synthetic and real-world-derived scenes demonstrate improved metric geometry and
leading visual quality on CitySample-Aerial (CS-Aerial) and UrbanScene3D among the evaluated
baselines. Ablations examine the contributions of depth adaptation, scale calibration, and structural
guidance in generation. We further evaluate continued city-state use across 50 task chains of 50
steps each, covering construction, editing, querying, planning, and historical restoration. All 2,500
steps pass their task checks under the documented execution protocol, with operations continuing
after restoration. Together, CityCode and Air2Code construct grounded urban worlds with accurate
metric geometry and leading visual quality, providing persistent, agent-operable environments for
long-horizon urban spatial intelligence.
2 RELATEDWORK
2.1 URBANMODELING ANDGENERATION
Design-driven and structure-conditioned generation.CityGenAgent, CityCraft, and MajutsuCity
synthesize programs, layouts, and assets from design specifications, supporting structured control
and editing (Liu et al., 2026; Deng et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025). CityX and UrbanWorld sup-
port map-conditioned construction; UrbanWorld2.0 adds street-view evidence for reality-aligned
assets (Zhang et al., 2024; Shang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b). CityDreamer, GaussianCity,
and CityDreamer4D use layouts and heights; CityDreamer4D supports dynamic traffic and navi-
gation (Xie et al., 2024; 2025; 2026). Sat2RealCity combines satellite appearance with building
footprints and heights (Wang et al., 2025c). Sat2City and Map2World use height and segment maps,
respectively (Hua et al., 2025; Chung et al., 2026). Structural conditions are synthesized or supplied.
Image-conditioned 3D scene generation.Sat2City v2 generates textured 3D city assets from
satellite imagery (Hua et al., 2026). Sat2Density++ learns radiance fields for satellite-to-street-
view synthesis, while Sat3DGen generates street-level scenes with mesh extraction (Qian et al.,
2026a;b). MagicCity reconstructs Gaussian scenes from generated views with instance-consistency
guidance (Yao et al., 2025). SceneFuse-3D combines regional generation and 3D inpainting from
a single top-down image (Zheng et al., 2025). These methods emphasize scene-level generation;
Air2Code constructs metric building instances, explicit road connectivity, and associated 3D assets
from sparse observations.
2.2 STRUCTUREDREPRESENTATIONS ANDSPATIALINTERACTION
CityGML 3.0 defines semantic urban objects and model versions (Open Geospatial Consortium,
2021). SpatialLM recovers indoor layouts and objects from point clouds (Mao et al., 2025). Hydra
and ConceptGraphs build 3D scene graphs (Hughes et al., 2022; Gu et al., 2023). SayPlan uses hier-
archical scene graphs and feedback for planning (Rana et al., 2023). LEO integrates 3D perception,
grounding, reasoning, and action (Huang et al., 2024). CityBench evaluates urban understanding
and decision-making (Feng et al., 2025). CityCube studies cross-view spatial reasoning (Xu et al.,
2026). MetaUrban provides urban environments for embodied navigation (Wu et al., 2025).
Code World Model separates code-maintained state evolution from proxy-conditioned visual gener-
ation (Chen et al., 2026). SceneCode links indoor-world programs and simulation assets through a
persistent registry for local editing (Wang et al., 2026). Recursive Code World Models reconstruct
image-conditioned 3D worlds through recursive scene programs (Li et al., 2026b). CityCode links
metric building instances, road connectivity, and assets to observations and supports version-bound
queries and plans after dependent edits and historical restoration.

relations encode building membership, neighborhoods, and road associations. Environment lay-
ers provide registered background imagery and available height and confidence fields. Structural
projections to OSM XML and CityJSON support map and semantic 3D interchange (Appendix H).

3.3 DEPENDENCY-AWAREVERSIONEDEXECUTION
CityCode supports tool-mediated interaction (Yao et al., 2023) over an evolving city by coupling
revision-bound queries with dependency-aware, validated edits (Figure 4). Operation schemas, de-
pendency rules, and recovery are detailed in Appendix F.
Revision-grounded interaction.Each request specifies an operation, arguments, and a read re-
vision. Queries leave that snapshot unchanged: measurements use its entity geometry, routing its
road graph, and visibility its transformed meshes. Edits must reference the current revision. Stale
requests are rejected with feedback for replanning. Results remain linked to the revision they read.
Dependency-aware updates.An edit produces a candidate updated through operation-specific
dependency rules. Building edits synchronize geometry, asset transforms, block membership, and
spatial relations; road edits update the graph, blocks, affected building associations, and background
layers. Applicable checks on geometry, road clearance, and preservation of unselected entities pre-
cede publication. If checks fail, validation feedback guides the caller in revising the candidate.
Appearance-index maintenance follows publication: geometric queries remain available, while se-
mantic queries requiring that index are withheld until it is ready and matches the published revision.
Historical restoration and continued execution.Accepted edits publish immutable snapshots
with receipts linking requests to their read and resulting revisions. Request identifiers prevent du-
plicate commits on retry. Historical queries inspect earlier snapshots; restoration verifies referenced
resources and publishes the selected historical state as a new revision, preserving intervening history.
Queries, edits, and plans can continue from the restored state.

4.2 CITY ANDBUILDINGGENERATIONQUALITY
CityCode ranks first on all seven CS-Aerial metrics and six of seven UrbanScene3D metrics (Ta-
ble 1). It ranks second on UrbanScene3D SSIM (0.286 vs. 0.290). CS-Aerial PSNR exceeds the
strongest baseline by 1.17 dB. Across both datasets and prompts, its VLM preference rates lead the
strongest baseline by 10.09–44.74 percentage points. Image metrics use 1,000 CS-Aerial and 217
UrbanScene3D views at1024×1024. VLM comparisons use 300 and 217 views, respectively.
CityCode more closely matches reference building massing, street layout, and appearance (Figure 3),
consistent with the quantitative results. Recovered structure and observed appearance jointly ground
generative completion in aerial evidence, while structure-aware 3D generation helps preserve build-
ing shape and proportions. Although UrbanWorld2.0 and Sat3DGen also use input imagery, their
outputs show weaker correspondence to the observed scene, suggesting less effective use of this
evidence to constrain generation. Ground regions also show simplified road and vegetation patterns
in UrbanWorld2.0 and blurred surface textures in Sat3DGen.
Building-level fidelity.We compare CityCode with MajutsuCity and UrbanWorld2.0 on 547
building instances. CityCode leads all appearance metrics, improving PSNR by 3.47 and 3.67 dB
and reducing foreground RGB MAE by 39.8% and 36.1%, respectively. These gains are consistent
with our completion strategy, which combines observed source and roof appearance with recovered
geometry to guide building proportions. Its 2.5D prism IoU is 0.737, slightly below MajutsuCity’s
0.746 but above UrbanWorld2.0’s 0.394 (Appendix G.2).

4.4 LONG-HORIZONURBANINTERACTION
Beyond the example in Figure 4, we evaluate CityCode on 50 task chains of 50 steps each, spanning
construction, editing, spatial queries, multimodal retrieval, planning, and historical restoration. Each
chain maintains its own evolving scene state. CityCode completes all 50 chains, with all 2,500 steps
passing their task checks. Protocols and artifact verification are detailed in Appendix G.7.
We also test the same 50 task chains on the data structures produced by UrbanWorld2.0 and Ma-
jutsuCity. The diagnostics expose gaps in task-level execution: UrbanWorld2.0’s adapted edits
show cross-object constraint violations, while MajutsuCity lacks required road-relation workflows.
Source inspection further finds no integrated workflow for restoring task-indexed city states in either
evaluated implementation.

5 CONCLUSION
We introduced CityCode and Air2Code to build grounded urban worlds from sparse aerial obser-
vations. Persistent identities and versioned execution link instance geometry, topology, and assets.
Experiments show substantially more accurate metric structure recovery and superior visual fidelity
to target cities than evaluated baselines. Across 50 task chains of 50 steps, these persistent, agent-
operable cities support long-horizon urban spatial intelligence.
AI USESTATEMENT
We used large language models to assist with language polishing, literature search and review, code
debugging, and checking final results. We also used GPT-5.6-sol as an agent for long-horizon task
execution.
