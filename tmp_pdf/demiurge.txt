ABSTRACT
Recent advancements in video diffusion models have substantially empowered
generative video rerendering, where novel-view videos can be synthesized from
a single input video, facilitating a broad spectrum of applications such as world
modeling, embodied intelligence, and robotic simulation. However, despite their
impressive photorealism, existing video rerendering methods typically treat each
camera trajectory in isolation, lacking the cross-trajectory consistency required
to form a coherent and traversable 4D world. To address this limitation, we
introduceDemiurge, a novel autoregressive video rerendering pipeline for nav-
igable 4D world generation. Built upon a dynamic 4D point-cloud memory
cache, Demiurge promotes cross-trajectory consistency throughfour key com-
ponents: 1)an appearance video diffusion model that rerenders photorealistic
novel-view videos along target camera trajectories conditioned on the 4D mem-
ory;2)a memory-consistent video depth completion model that aligns known-
region depths with cached geometry while completing missing regions, mitigat-
ing scale ambiguity and discontinuities during memory updates;3)a memory-
aware dual-stream warping strategy that provides high-fidelity visual guidance
and occlusion cues for plausible novel-view video synthesis;4)a geometry-aware
video refinement model that further enhances the perceptual quality of synthe-
sized videos. Extensive experiments show that Demiurge achieves state-of-the-art
performance withthree key capabilities: 1)coherent spatiotemporal lattice con-
struction over dense multi-view trajectories;2)large-parallax video rerendering
under challenging camera motions, such as180 ◦ rotations with no input-view
overlap;3)geometry-faithful outputs that can be directly optimized into high-
quality 3D Gaussian Splatting representations with minimal artifacts. The project
page is available athttps://hanxinzhu-lab.github.io/demiurge_
project_page/.
1 INTRODUCTION
As a fundamental medium for representing evolving visual content, videos provide a natural record
of real-world dynamics, serving as a cornerstone for information sharing, content creation, and scene
understanding Tong et al. (2022); Ho et al. (2022). However, despite such informative spatiotempo-
ral cues, a single video alone still falls short of fully representing the underlying physical world, as
it provides only 2D projections captured along a specific camera trajectory, preserving partial obser-
vations of the scene structure while leaving occluded regions unobserved. This inherent limitation
has spurred growing interest in generative video rerendering, which aims to synthesize novel-view
videos from a single input video along target camera trajectories, offering a promising pathway to-
wards world modeling Yu et al. (2024; 2025b), embodied intelligence Tu et al. (2026), and robotic
simulation Zhou et al. (2024).
To achieve this goal, leveraging recent advances in video diffusion models, existing methods typi-
cally adopt a camera-conditioned video generation paradigm, incorporating target camera trajecto-

To move beyond trajectory-wise synthesis towards navigable 4D world construction, as shown in
Fig. 1(b), we introduceDemiurge, an autoregressive video rerendering framework underpinned by
a dynamic 4D point-cloud memory. At each autoregressive step, a novel-view video is synthesized
along a target camera trajectory using the current memory as conditioning, with its appearance and
recovered geometry subsequently fused back into the 4D memory to guide subsequent rerender-
ing and thus maintain cross-trajectory consistency. Building upon this formulation, as shown in
Fig. 2,Demiurge comprises four key components: 1) a memory-guided appearance video dif-
fusion modelthat synthesizes photorealistic novel-view videos along target camera trajectories us-
ing point-cloud renderings from the 4D memory, complemented by a lightweight noise-monitoring
mechanism that detects and suppresses conspicuous outliers in the rendered guidance to provide
cleaner conditioning signals;2) a memory-consistent video depth completion modelthat recovers
temporally coherent dense depth maps for each synthesized novel-view video under the guidance of

partial depth renderings from the 4D memory, anchoring predictions in previously observed regions
to cached metric geometry while completing newly revealed regions, thereby allowing the recovered
depths to be back-projected into dynamic point clouds and reliably fused into the memory for ge-
ometrically consistent memory updates;3) a memory-aware dual-stream warping strategythat
derives high-fidelity appearance guidance from a selectively updated point cloud and reliable occlu-
sion cues from a densely updated counterpart, fusing the two according to their depth discrepancies
to preserve stable visual content and guide plausible synthesis in newly exposed regions, thereby
suppressing geometric artifacts and cumulative color drift over autoregressive iterations;4) an op-
tional geometry-aware video refinement modelthat leverages temporally consistent target-view
depth maps alongside partial RGB renderings to attenuate subtle texture flicker and noise, yielding
refined outputs with enhanced visual fidelity and temporal coherence.
Extensive evaluations spanning diverse scenes and viewing paths demonstrate that Demiurge attains
state-of-the-art results in perceptual realism, geometric accuracy, and cross-trajectory consistency.
Notably, it deliversthree capabilitiesessential to navigable 4D worlds:1)assembling coherent
spatiotemporal lattices over densely sampled viewpoints;2)sustaining robust rerendering under
extreme parallax, including180◦ rotations without input-view overlap; and3)producing geometry-
faithful videos that can be directly optimized into high-quality 3D Gaussian Splatting representations
with minimal artifacts.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We proposeDemiurge, an autoregressive video rerendering framework with a dynamic 4D point-
cloud memory for cross-trajectory-consistent and navigable 4D world generation.
• To ensure high-fidelity synthesis and consistent memory evolution,we introduce four coor-
dinated designs:an appearance video diffusion model for photorealistic novel-view synthesis;
a video depth completion model for reliable geometry recovery and memory updates; a dual-
stream warping strategy for appearance-preserving occlusion handling without cumulative color
drift; and an optional video refinement model for attenuating texture flicker and noise.
• Extensive experiments demonstrate that Demiurge achieves state-of-the-art perceptual realism
and cross-trajectory consistency, supports robust large-parallax rerendering, and yields geometry-
faithful outputs for high-quality 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction.
2 RELATEDWORKS
2.1 CAMERA-CONTROLLABLEVIDEOGENERATION
Early methods condition video diffusion on parameterized camera poses, either through a dedicated
camera-motion module Wang et al. (2024) or via pixel-aligned Pl ¨ucker ray embeddings He et al.
(2024); Xu et al. (2024); Bahmani et al. (2025a;b); He et al. (2025); Zheng et al. (2024). However,
since viewpoint transformations are learned implicitly, they are prone to geometric distortion and
content drift under large camera motion. Recently, geometry-grounded methods Gu et al. (2025);
Ma et al. (2025) instead lift source observations into 3D and reproject them into target views. For
example, ViewCrafter Yu et al. (2024) and GEN3C Ren et al. (2025) condition on partial point-cloud
renderings, FlexWorld Chen et al. (2025b) and V oyager Huang et al. (2025) progressively expand
persistent 3D scenes with newly generated observations, and GTA Zhu et al. (2026) decouples ge-
ometry generation from appearance synthesis. More recently, camera control has been repurposed as
a generative renderer for dynamic scenes Zheng et al. (2026); Yang et al. (2026); Song et al. (2026);
Lin et al. (2026); Park et al. (2026), re-rendering monocular videos along novel trajectories while
preserving their original dynamics, either from encoded extrinsics Bai et al. (2025) or by explicitly
warping the source video with estimated geometry Yu et al. (2025b). Both, however, synthesize
each trajectory independently and retain nothing for subsequent views. In contrast, Demiurge main-
tains an explicit 4D memory of generated appearance and recovered geometry, enforcing long-range
consistency across diverse camera trajectories.
2.2 MEMORY-AUGMENTEDVIDEOGENERATION
To mitigate context forgetting and visual drift in long-horizon generation, one line of work retrieves
or reuses historical frames according to their relevance to the current state Xiao et al. (2026); Yu
et al. (2025a); Schneider et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2025a), while another compresses the gener-
ation history into hierarchical, multi-scale, or sliding-window latent memories Liu et al. (2026);
Li et al. (2025a); Mao et al. (2025); Cai et al. (2026); Team et al. (2026). Such appearance- and

feature-space memories offer limited explicit geometric grounding, causing spatial drift under large
camera motion. Geometry-aware approaches instead organize history in explicit 3D space, using a
surfel-indexed view memory Li et al. (2025b), point-cloud spatial context Wu et al. (2026), an in-
crementally expanded RGB-D cache Huang et al. (2025), or a persistent point cloud with dynamic–
static separation Zhao et al. (2026); yet they predominantly preserve static geometry rather than the
complete time-varying scene state. Most closely related to ours, PlenopticDreamer Fu et al. (2026)
banks previously generated video–camera pairs and retrieves the top-kby 3D field-of-view overlap.
Its fixed context capacity, however, forces selective retrieval or trajectory fusion once the number of
trajectories exceedsk, discarding fine-grained detail and tying long-range consistency to retrieval
quality. In contrast, Demiurge consolidates reliable appearance and recovered geometry from all
generated trajectories into a unified 4D memory, avoiding a fixed video-context bottleneck.

3.2 MEMORY-CONSISTENTVIDEODEPTHCOMPLETION
Given the generated novel-view RGB videoV i, to incorporate its newly observed content into the
4D memory and support the next autoregressive step, we need to recover a dense video depth that is
both temporally coherent and geometrically consistent with the existing memory. A straightforward
solution is to apply an off-the-shelf monocular video depth estimator toV i. However, monocular

over subsequent iterations. To address this issue, we therefore recover dense geometry from the

3.3 MEMORY-AWAREDUAL-STREAMWARPING
After recovering the RGB-D video(Vi,Di), we next consider how to update the point-cloud mem-
ory so that it can provide reliable conditions for the subsequent target trajectory. A natural strategy
is to register only newly generated pixels while retaining the points accumulated in previously ob-
served regions. Such a selective update prevents stable colors from being repeatedly overwritten
5
Preprint
by diffusion-generated appearance, thereby preserving sharp and visually consistent renderings over
successive autoregressive iterations.
However, selective updates may leave the point cloud insufficiently dense for reliable visibility rea-
soning (as shown in the leftmost RGB–depth pair of Fig. 4(d) ). Under large viewpoint changes,
sparse foreground points may fail to fully occlude the background during z-buffer rendering, caus-
ing background content that should remain hidden to leak into foreground regions. This failure is
not captured by the visibility mask, which only records whether a pixel originates from cached or
newly generated content, rather than whether its projection remains geometrically valid under the
target viewpoint. Consequently, these erroneous projections may be treated as reliable appearance
guidance, propagated into the diffusion output, and eventually written back into the memory.
An alternative is to register all pixels from each generated RGB-D video. The resulting dense point
cloud provides more complete foreground geometry and enables more reliable occlusion reason-
ing under large parallax. Nevertheless, this strategy also repeatedly incorporates the generated ap-
pearance of previously observed regions. Small texture deviations and high-frequency artifacts can
therefore accumulate over multiple iterations, eventually producing blurred renderings and color
drift (as shown in the middle RGB–depth pair of Fig. 4(d)).

3.4 GEOMETRY-AWAREVIDEOREFINEMENT
With the novel-view RGB videoVi, its dense depth videoD i, and the memory update mechanism
described above, the core autoregressive loop is already sufficient to support the generation of sub-
sequent target trajectories. However, we observe thatV i may occasionally exhibit subtle texture
flicker and high-frequency noise, which degrade its perceptual quality (as shown in Fig. 4(c)). For-
tunately, our video depth completion model remains robust to these mild appearance artifacts and
produces temporally stable and spatially smooth depth predictions. This observation motivates an
optional geometry-aware refinement stage that leverages the recovered depth as a reliable structural
prior to further improve the generated appearance.

4.3 ABLATION STUDIES
Tab. 3 and Fig. 4 validate the complementary roles of our components. The noise monitor sup-
presses unreliable memory projections and the resulting boundary artifacts. Replacing video depth
completion with monocular depth estimation increases P-MSE from 0.0010 to 0.0020 and decreases
View Sync from 64.893 to 54.226, demonstrating the importance of memory-consistent geometry
recovery. Dual-stream warping contributes most to cross-trajectory consistency: removing it raises
P-MSE to 0.0049 and reduces View Sync to 51.498. As visualized in Fig. 4(d), its appearance
and geometry streams respectively preserve visual details and reliable occlusion geometry. Finally,
geometry-aware refinement further removes residual artifacts and improves local details. Together,
these components yield high-quality generation with consistent appearance and geometry. Addi-
tional visualizations are provided in Appendix G.
5 CONCLUSION
We introducedDemiurge, an autoregressive video rerendering framework for constructing navi-
gable 4D worlds from monocular videos. Unlike existing methods that synthesize each camera
trajectory independently, Demiurge continuously consolidates generated appearance and recovered
geometry into an evolving 4D point-cloud memory through a generate–recover–update cycle. This
explicit memory enables reliable reuse of historical observations and preserves long-range consis-

perceptual quality, trajectory accuracy, and cross-trajectory consistency, as well as demonstrating
robust large-parallax rerendering and geometry-faithful 3DGS reconstruction.

learning rate of1×10 −4, and a weight decay of1×10 −2. The learning rate follows a cosine
annealing schedule without warm-up and decreases to1%of its initial value, i.e.,1×10 −6, over the
complete training process.
All models are trained for five epochs using bfloat16 precision. Each training sample contains 81
frames at a resolution of480×832. We use a per-GPU batch size of one on eight NVIDIA H20
GPUs, resulting in a global batch size of eight without additional gradient accumulation. Each train-
ing sample is used once per epoch. We enable distributed data parallelism with unused-parameter
detection and save the trainable video-DiT parameters every 1,000 optimization steps. The module-
specific inputs and supervision targets follow the training-data construction described in Sec. C.
C.3 INFERENCECONFIGURATIONS
At inference time, Demiurge sequentially processes the prescribed target camera trajectories. For
each trajectory, we first render the current 4D memory using dual-stream warping and remove unre-
liable pixels using the noise monitor. The cleaned partial RGB, depth, and visibility conditions are
then passed toF app to generate the initial novel-view RGB videoV i. Subsequently,F dep predicts
a dense relative disparity, which is converted to metric depth through the two-stage global align-
ment and Poisson refinement. Finally,F ref uses the partial RGB rendering and completed dense
depth to produce the refined videoV i
ref. The resulting RGB-D pair(V i
ref,Di)is back-projected
and accumulated into the 4D memory before processing the next target trajectory.
All three video diffusion modules generate 81-frame videos at a resolution of480×832. We use 50
flow-matching inference steps. All diffusion modules operate in bfloat16 precision. We enable tiled
Wan V AE encoding and decoding with a tile size of(30,52)and a tile stride of(15,26)to reduce
peak memory consumption.
To control peak memory during multi-source rendering, we process at most 16 consecutive frames
and eight historical trajectories in each rendering batch. This chunking only changes the execution
order and does not affect the z-buffer composition result. Rendered metric depth is encoded as
bounded inverse depth usingϕ(d) = 1/(d+ 1)before being used as a diffusion condition. The
geometric depth is recovered when the observation is subsequently used for point-cloud construction
or warping.

J RUNTIMEANALYSIS
Under our default inference setting, the appearance video diffusion, video depth completion, and
optional geometry-aware refinement modules each require approximately 1.5 minutes to process
an 81-frame video at a resolution of480×832. With refinement enabled, generating one target
trajectory takes approximately 5 minutes end to end. Since target trajectories are processed autore-
gressively, the total inference time scales approximately linearly with their number, i.e., about5N
minutes forNtrajectories. Refinement can be disabled when faster inference is preferred, reducing
the diffusion inference time to approximately 3 minutes per trajectory. Although the current imple-
mentation is not optimized for speed, Demiurge remains stable over substantially longer generation

K LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
The primary limitation of Demiurge is its inference latency. The current pipeline comprises several
sequentially coupled modules, including appearance generation, video depth completion, optional
geometry-aware refinement, and 4D memory update. Their intermediate RGB, depth, and geometric
representations must be exchanged and synchronized before the next autoregressive step, prevent-
ing straightforward parallel execution. Consequently, the full pipeline requires approximately five
minutes to generate one target-trajectory video, which limits its applicability to interactive scenarios.
An important direction for future work is to decouple latency-sensitive RGB generation from back-
ground geometry recovery and memory maintenance through an asynchronous cloud–edge architec-
ture. Since users only consume the final RGB video, a fast path could immediately query the latest
4D cache and relevant video memories to synthesize the requested view, while a slower background
path performs depth completion, optional refinement, back-projection, and memory updates asyn-
chronously. The fast path would support responsive rendering within the currently cached region,
whereas the background path would continuously consolidate newly generated observations and ex-
pand the renderable world. Such a fast–slow design could substantially reduce perceived latency
without discarding the geometric consistency provided by the complete generate–recover–update
cycle.
Moreover, developing a more efficient representation of the evolving 4D memory is equally im-
portant. Our current time indexed point-cloud representation prioritizes explicitness and geometric
interpretability, but repeated observations may introduce redundant points and increase storage, re-
trieval, and rendering costs as the number of trajectories grows. Future work could jointly align and
compress historical observations into a hierarchical spatiotemporal representation, merging geomet-
rically and temporally compatible content while preserving dynamic boundaries and newly revealed
regions. Confidence-aware fusion, adaptive spatial discretization, and compact 4D primitives may
further reduce memory overhead and accelerate memory queries, enabling longer and larger-scale
world expansion.
