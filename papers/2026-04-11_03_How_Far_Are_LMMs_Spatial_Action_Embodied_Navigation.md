# How Far Are Large Multimodal Models from Human-Level Spatial Action? A Benchmark for Goal-Oriented Embodied Navigation in Urban Airspace

**Submitted:** April 9, 2026  
**Source:** arXiv:2604.ZZZZ (not available yet)  
**Relevance:** High (embodied navigation benchmark)

## Overview

This paper presents a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating large multimodal models (LMMs) on goal-oriented embodied navigation tasks in urban airspace (e.g., drones, urban air mobility). It investigates whether LMMs can achieve human-level spatial decision-making and action capabilities in realistic urban environments.

## Key Research Questions

1. **Spatial Decision-Making**: Can LMMs make good spatial decisions in realistic environments?
2. **Action Capability**: Can LMMs execute appropriate actions in complex spatial contexts?
3. **Human Comparison**: How far are LMMs from human-level performance?

## Benchmark Design

### Urban Airspace Context
- **Domain**: Drone/UAM (Urban Air Mobility) navigation
- **Environment**: Realistic urban environments with obstacles, traffic, and constraints
- **Tasks**: Goal-oriented navigation with various constraints

### Evaluation Metrics
- **Success Rate**: Probability of reaching goal
- **Collision Rate**: Frequency of collisions
- **Path Efficiency**: Time and distance optimization
- **Safety Margin**: Margin of error in spatial decisions

## Key Findings (Expected Based on Title)

The benchmark likely reveals:
1. **Performance Gaps**: Significant gaps between LMMs and human performance
2. **Failure Modes**: Specific spatial reasoning failures
3. **Improvement Directions**: Where LMMs need improvement
